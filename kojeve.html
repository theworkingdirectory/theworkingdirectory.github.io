Book miniatures: Kojève, Hegel

Another book I own. It pretends to be a commentary on Hegel’s Phenomenology of Spirit but is really a blend of Hegel, Nietzsche and Heidegger. The main reason to read this book, and the reason I own it, is that it ended up being the single most influential interpretation of Hegel in France, and everyone from Sartre to Lacan and Bataille means Kojève when they say Hegel. So for anyone with a passing interest in post-Heideggerian French philosophy, which includes myself, this is a helpful text to be familiar with. It is also painfully repetitive, serious Hegel people will tell you that it is an abominable distortion of the Phenomenology of Spirit, and while the discussion of dialectics and of the master-slave relationship is remarkably lucid, I maintain that Chapter 3 cannot be made sense of, which bugs me because I wasted a whole lot of time trying.

I hadn’t read this in years, and I haven’t read a whole lot of quote-unquote serious philosophy at all recently. I felt I understood both more and less than I used to. The argument that Kojève makes became clearer to me, but I have somehow lost my sense of why this text exists. Reading it felt like joining a conversation that has been going on for a while, and while you can follow what is being said, you don’t understand anymore what’s at stake and why anyone would care, and everything seems to rest on a million presuppositions that you don’t share. The question is if you want to try to join the conversation or just move on with your life. I haven’t decided yet if I’ll keep the book.
