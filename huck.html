Book miniatures: Twain, Huckleberry Finn

This is the second much-loved children’s classic I’ve read recently in the hope of finding in it some easy-going feel-good nostalgia, after Lewis’ The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe. As mentioned in my previous post, I found Lewis largely insufferable, but I unconditionally approve of Adventures of Huckleberry Finn. I literally cannot remember the last time I had so much fun with a book. Anyone who hasn’t read it has made a big mistake. Huge.

I was wondering why my reaction to these two books was so different, given that they are both on some level morality tales for children. What stood out to me when reading Huckleberry Finn for the first time as an adult was the depiction of utter poverty and ignorance. We see any imaginable quackery and grifting, a worldview so narrow and intolerant that it descends into lynching on a regular basis, decisions being made on the flimsiest of grounds. This is in stark contrast to Narnia. It feels like Twain, unlike Lewis, actually took his readers seriously: he didn’t just give them a story of good triumphing over evil, but a story about how messed up the world is, and he left it to the reader to fix it. The only reasons why all of this is still entertaining as hell is that, first, the world is messed up to an extent that ends up being utterly ridiculous and hilarious, which is true when you think about it, and second, Twain seems to have had a fair amount of trust in his readers to actually do something about it.
