Book miniatures: Hao, Empire of AI

I’m badly behind with book miniatures, so the next four will be reduced to the absolute minimum.

There are arguably many ways of reading this book, but I read it as a tale of ambition. It tells the story of OpenAI and its de facto leader, Sam Altman. OpenAI was originally founded as a non-profit organization with the goal of creating AI that would benefit rather than harm humanity. This task was all the more imperative, Altman and others claimed, because the rise of AI was inevitable either way, and unless we stepped up to build benign AI, the Chinese would step in to build evil AI that would take over the world, HAL-style. As Hao points out, this narrative makes no sense. Creating ChatGPT-level AI required vast amounts of resources – in terms of funding, technology, political influence – that no other entity, whether commercial or governmental, would have obtained. If OpenAI had not created the product that it did, nobody else would have for a very long time.

Unsurprisingly, OpenAI has come a long way since its non-profit beginnings, evolving into a conventional Silicon Valley company that does not benefit humanity in particularly obvious ways. It is hard to say whether its founders genuinely believed in their initial lofty ideas or whether these were only ever a cover-up. If something becomes clear in Hao’s telling of this story, it is that the rise of OpenAI was largely fuelled by Altman’s sheer drive and relentless pursuit of success. Having lived in this country for five years now, I feel that my perspective on this has shifted. On some level, I still manage to be appropriately outraged by the ways in which Altman tricks, schemes and bullies OpenAI into power, by any means and against all odds, at the cost of exploiting data workers and leaving a huge carbon footprint. (In true degenerate liberal fashion, my first response to reading this book was to delete my ChatGPT account so as to excuse myself from any personal responsibility.) But it also gave me a bona fide adrenaline rush. He is so successful. When I was younger, I never understood why anyone would get hooked onto power for its own sake, but I do now. There is something so energizing and exhilarating about striving for success, which makes you do things you didn’t think you could do, and perhaps shouldn’t do, and it all makes me slightly afraid of myself.
